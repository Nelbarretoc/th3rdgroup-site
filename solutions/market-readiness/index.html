BEFORE DEFINING THE SCOPE

Questions to prepare your next step.

Does a cosmetic product need FDA approval, registration or listing?

These are different questions. FDA explains that most cosmetic products are not subject to premarket approval; some ingredients, such as color additives, have separate requirements. Product classification, claims and the applicable MoCRA provisions still need review. Confirm the product, business roles, market and evidence before treating a registration, listing or review as clearance. TH3RD’s preliminary assessment is not an FDA approval, filing service or legal determination.

  • FDA: cosmetics and approval
  • FDA: MoCRA
How are packaging, labels and ingredient statements reviewed?

Identify the exact SKU, current artwork for every panel, dimensions, intended use, ingredient statement, recorded claims and target country. A label/INCI review compares available evidence with the applicable source context; it does not infer a formula, missing text or approval from an incomplete photo. Separate “not observed,” missing evidence and a supported finding. Keep a tracked correction brief and request specialist input where product classification, ingredient restrictions or other requirements require it.

  • FDA: cosmetics labeling
Can a skincare claim change the review the product needs?

The exact wording, intended use and surrounding product presentation matter. Review the package, website, listings and campaign language together with the supporting records. FDA’s guidance distinguishes cosmetic and drug-related claim questions; a phrase cannot be approved in isolation from that context. Keep source quotations separate from TH3RD’s interpretation and from proposed replacement wording. A preliminary review does not create an approved claims list or replace qualified legal and regulatory judgment.

  • FDA: cosmetics labeling claims
What documents should be organized before export, import or a retailer review?

Begin with the confirmed product identity, current labels, ingredients, claim support, responsible business parties, intended market/channel and available technical records. Record source, version, receipt and review status separately. A document checklist is country- and product-specific; it is not proof that the underlying product is compliant. A readiness report should identify missing evidence, supported findings, accountable next steps and where testing, submissions or specialist review may be needed before a commercial commitment.

Can the same cosmetic documentation be used in Colombia, Mexico and Brazil?

Do not assume the requirements or responsible-party roles are interchangeable. INVIMA/NSO questions in Colombia, COFEPRIS context in Mexico and ANVISA regularization/labeling in Brazil need the appropriate official sources and product-specific review. Establish destination, classification, local roles and the exact document version first. TH3RD can organize preliminary questions and source evidence within agreed scope; this is not a filing, authorization or representation before any authority.

  • INVIMA: cosméticos
  • COFEPRIS: productos cosméticos
  • ANVISA: cosméticos
How should a California Proposition 65 question be handled?

Treat a California warning question as a separate scope, not an automatic extension of a federal cosmetic review. Identify the exact product, available formulation and exposure information, destination and the decision that requires qualified review. Do not infer a warning obligation or absence of risk from a keyword, ingredient name or an empty checklist. Use OEHHA’s official information and qualified advice for the applicable evaluation; the preliminary readiness report is not a Proposition 65 clearance.

  • OEHHA: Proposition 65
